## Decisions taken

- **Changed English strings keep their keys and get new Danish from the session.** The task
  forbids changing `da.json` without review, meaning the review of the existing Danish for
  tone and terminology; the empty states and next-step errors are new sentences (the English
  changed), and `da.json` must be complete for every string. So they were written, and every
  one is listed under *Danish review → New and changed strings* for the reviewer. Only two
  changes to *unchanged* English strings were made, both consistency (table below).
- **The sizes are 18 / 21 / 24 px**, factors 1.17 and 1.33: large is the step a tired reader
  wants; largest is a metre away. Everything is in rem, so the whole screen scales. Applied
  at once rather than on Save (like the language): the operator must see it to choose it.
- **High contrast keeps navy, red and green.** The task says the greys go to black and white;
  the brand navy (11.5:1 on white), the error red (8.8:1) and the ok green (8.4:1) are not
  greys, pass, and keep the status words' second cue. The tinted rows, the notice and band
  backgrounds go white and get black borders instead.
- **Table row dividers are `--rule`, not checked**; field, panel and table-head borders are
  `--line` at 3:1. WCAG's 3:1 is for the boundaries of components the operator acts on; a
  hairline between two rows is decoration, and a 3:1 grey on every row makes the table heavy.
  In high contrast `--rule` is black too.
- **The radio dots are not in the pair list**; they got a border and sit beside their word.
  A filled dot is a cue, not a boundary; its border is `--on-navy` in the bar and `--ink` on
  the handover panel, both in the checked set.
- **The pairs are declared in the script, by name and purpose**, not inferred from the CSS:
  the check says which text sits on which surface, and a new pairing is one line in `PAIRS`
  (README). The script is plain JavaScript with a `.d.mts` so Node 22 (CI) runs it without a
  loader and vitest imports the same module.
- **Ten rows means ten one-line rows.** With 18 px, 1.4 and a two-line message row (text plus
  strip) a 60 vh box on a 720 px screen holds seven message rows; ten of those would need the
  timeline to be the whole screen or the strip to go. The box now ends inside the viewport
  (the `h1` in the bar, the timeline heading hidden), which it did not before at 18 px.
  *Open questions*.
- **The journal's `h1` is in the incident bar** rather than visually hidden: a page keeps its
  visible heading; the bar is the page's head anyway. The "Timeline" `h2` is hidden visually
  (the table under it is self-evident) and stays as the section's label.
- **`R` replies to the current row**, which is the focused row when a row has the focus, else
  the cursor row set by `J` / `K` or the last focus. No cursor → nothing (no guessing at
  "the newest incoming row"). `Enter` only acts when the row itself has the focus, so Enter on
  a button or a link inside a row is that element's.
- **The resolver is pure and the wiring thin**, so "fires outside a text field and not
  inside one" is a table of cases, not a DOM test; the DOM wiring (focus, scroll, the
  dialog) is by hand (*Open questions*). No component test harness was added (a dependency
  decision, P1-02 follow-up stands).
- **Ctrl+Enter only; Meta+Enter is not send.** The task names Ctrl+Enter; on macOS ⌘+Enter
  would be a second spelling of the same thing — one line in `resolveShortcut` if wanted
  (*Follow-ups*).
- **The G prefix lasts 2 s** and a wrong second key ends the chord without firing that key's
  own action: `G` `N` does not open a new message. The window listener, not each page, keeps
  the state, so the chord works across a navigation.
- **Forms keep their own Esc and Ctrl+Enter** (compose: drop the link / send; manual: add) and
  mark the key taken with `preventDefault`; the window listener then leaves it alone. Esc
  with no link to drop is not taken by the form and falls through to the timeline's "close
  the open row form" and the overlay.
- **The overlay is a `<dialog>`**: `showModal()` gives the focus trap, the backdrop and the
  Esc for free; a click on the backdrop closes it. The **?** button in the top bar makes the
  set discoverable without knowing the key.
- **Two-line top bar.** At 1280 px and 18 px the single flex line overflowed (and did at
  16 px already) whenever a notice showed; the notices are the things that must be read, so
  they get the line under the bar, full width, with a border so they read in high contrast.
- **The handover lists go to one column under 80 rem**, not under 60 rem as before: at
  1280 px two columns of 610 px cannot hold a table with three row buttons.
- **Chip glyphs through CSS `content`**, not through `t()`: symbols, the same in both
  languages, no catalog entry to translate.
- **Prettier** was run on the new files and on the touched files that were formatted before;
  `TopBar.svelte` and `ManualEntryForm.svelte` were unformatted before and were rewritten
  wholesale, so they are formatted now; `settings.svelte.ts`, `settings.test.ts`,
  `JournalPage.svelte`, `SettingsPage.svelte`, `ContactPicker.svelte`, `AdhocBatchForm.svelte`
  and `README.md` were unformatted before and are left so (P1-01 follow-up stands). Three
  files came out of the editor as CRLF (`ContactPicker.svelte`, `settings.svelte.ts`,
  `settings.test.ts`) and were turned back to LF before the commit.
- Candidates for `docs/decisions.md`: every screen colour is a token checked at 7:1 / 3:1 in
  two modes by the test run; one documented keyboard set, nothing in a text field but
  Ctrl+Enter and Esc; the base type is 18 px / 1.4 with three sizes.
